# Pilot and product validation protocol

This is a prepared protocol, not a report of completed recruitment or measured
benefit. No participants, interviews, willingness-to-pay results, or partner
commitments have been fabricated. Do not contact people without the founder's
explicit instruction and intended recipients.

## Before physical sessions

Use one measured board revision, specified parts, reviewed current-limiting and
polarity assumptions, and a current-limited supply. Do not energize the bundled
unverified reference layouts. Obtain institution/educator review, participation
consent, and separate photo-training consent. Include deletion/retention procedures.
Do not collect student names in free-text pilot notes; use pseudonymous participant
IDs in a separately controlled study roster.

## Interview 20–30 students/teaching assistants

Ask about the last actual circuit-building experience:

- What were you trying to build, and which tools/instructions did you use?
- Show the point where you got stuck. What did you try next?
- How did you discover whether the circuit was wired correctly?
- How much time and help did it take? What evidence supports that estimate?
- Which existing workaround would you lose if you changed tools?
- Who chooses and pays for lab tools? Have you paid for a similar tool before?

Avoid asking whether a hypothetical AI tool sounds useful. Record concrete behavior,
failure patterns, and quotes with consent, separating interpretation from evidence.

## Controlled assembly pilot

Aim for at least 15 participants across the initial audience; treat this as an
exploratory sample, not a powered claim. Use matched small circuits and counterbalance
guided/control order to reduce learning effects. Keep board, parts, instructions,
lighting, and assistance rules consistent. Define success criteria before sessions.

The app's **Data & pilot** panel records condition, start/end time, observed assembly
error count, and help requests. The observer enters counts; the app does not infer
errors automatically. Account owners export only their own measurements. Abandoned
sessions remain incomplete rather than being assigned a fabricated completion time.

Record first successful assembly, incorrect insertions, time to success, assistance,
photo false alarms, misses, occlusions, calibration failures, and user understanding
of uncertainty. Analyze paired time/error differences with participant-level
uncertainty. Keep unsuccessful attempts in the report. The 30% time reduction target
is aspirational until supported by actual results.

## Commercial validation and go/no-go

Test commitment through opted-in pilot signups, institutional meetings, or actual
paid-trial intent with clear terms. Do not infer willingness to pay from compliments.
Compare current alternatives and verify any integration/licensing claims. Pricing
in the handoff remains a hypothesis. Research naming/trademark availability before
commercial launch; no clearance is claimed by this implementation.

Proceed only when physical correctness gates are met, error rates are understood,
users interpret uncertainty appropriately, and evidence indicates a useful workflow.
Document remaining failures and the resulting continue/change/stop decision.
