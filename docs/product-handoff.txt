1. PRODUCT VISION AND USERS
======================================================================
Name: CircuitPilot (working name; check name/trademark availability before commercial launch).
One-liner: An AI-powered assembly copilot that turns a digital circuit design into step-by-step breadboard instructions and helps users verify physical wiring with photos.

Problem: Beginners spend excessive time translating circuit diagrams to physical breadboards and debugging wiring mistakes. Circuit simulators and diagram tools do not necessarily guide or verify physical assembly.

Initial users: engineering/polytechnic students, electronics educators, Arduino beginners and hobbyists. First validation audience: students in introductory electronics labs.

Differentiation hypothesis (must validate): CircuitPilot is the bridge between a logical circuit and its real physical implementation, rather than another simulator. It offers precise hole coordinates, guided assembly, and camera-based discrepancy checks.

Two long-term product directions:
A. Digital schematic/netlist -> physical assembly guidance and verification (MVP priority).
B. Photograph messy wired breadboard -> digital circuit reconstruction (later; much harder because occluded wires and uncertain connections).

Do NOT make arbitrary Tinkercad screenshot recognition or direct editable Tinkercad project creation an MVP dependency. Start with CircuitPilot JSON and later a documented structured format such as Wokwi diagram.json, subject to compatibility and terms review.

======================================================================
2. MVP SCOPE AND NON-GOALS
======================================================================
MVP end-to-end workflow:
1. User creates a project and imports a supported structured circuit/netlist.
2. Backend validates components, pin references, nets, supply requirements, and basic electrical rules.
3. User selects one supported breadboard model (initially a verified 830-tie-point model).
4. Planner assigns components and jumpers to physical holes, avoiding shorts and respecting footprints.
5. Frontend shows a clickable SVG breadboard, numbered placement instructions, and highlighted endpoints.
6. User takes a top-down photo of the empty/partially wired board.
7. Vision pipeline aligns the board and, in later MVP iterations, detects visible components and wire endpoints.
8. Verification compares observations with expected layout and flags probable mismatches, uncertainties, and occlusions; it never asserts full electrical continuity from a photograph.

Initial components: resistor, LED, push button, jumper wire, battery/supply terminal; optionally add potentiometer, simple transistor, and a common sensor after testing. Start with circuits of 2-8 components and 1-10 jumpers. Use a small curated circuit library.

Non-goals for v0.1: arbitrary IC layouts, high-voltage/mains circuits, all breadboard types, full analog simulation, live AR, guaranteed fault diagnosis, photo-only reconstruction of heavily tangled circuits, native Tinkercad write integration, custom hardware.

Safety: support only low-voltage, current-limited, unpowered-while-wiring educational assemblies. Show a disconnect-power reminder. Check LED polarity, resistor current limiting, supply polarity, and likely power shorts. Do not imply camera inspection proves circuit safety.

======================================================================
3. ENGINEERING PRINCIPLES
======================================================================
- The logical netlist is the source of truth for intended connectivity.
- The physical placement graph is the source of truth for planned conductive connections.
- Vision produces uncertain observations, not electrical truth.
- The LLM explains validated results but cannot bypass the circuit validator.
- Every supported released layout must pass deterministic graph checks.
- Version circuit inputs and generated plans; never silently mutate historical results.
- Make assumptions explicit, especially breadboard power-rail splits and component footprints.
- Build a modular monolith first; defer microservices and complex cloud infrastructure.
- Prefer small, testable modules and deterministic behavior with seeded planning.

5. DATA MODEL: LOGICAL CIRCUIT
======================================================================
Use a versioned JSON schema. Core objects:
- Component: id, type, value/parameters, pin names, footprint reference, orientation constraints.
- Net: id, list of component-pin references, optional role (power, ground, signal).
- Supply: voltage/current limit and terminal net references, if applicable.
- Circuit metadata: schema_version, name, units, assumptions.

Illustrative logical circuit (5V -> resistor -> LED -> ground):
{
  "schema_version": "1.0",
  "name": "Series LED",
  "components": [
    {"id":"R1","type":"resistor","value_ohms":220,"pins":["1","2"]},
    {"id":"D1","type":"led","pins":["anode","cathode"]}
  ],
  "nets": [
    {"id":"VCC","members":["R1.1"],"role":"power"},
    {"id":"N1","members":["R1.2","D1.anode"],"role":"signal"},
    {"id":"GND","members":["D1.cathode"],"role":"ground"}
  ],
  "supply":{"voltage_v":5.0,"current_limit_ma":100,"positive_net":"VCC","negative_net":"GND"}
}

Interpretation: R1.2 and D1.anode must be electrically connected. A logical net may have one listed component pin if it also represents an external supply terminal. Reject undeclared pins, duplicate conflicting pin assignments, unsupported values/footprints, and missing required supply terminal mapping.

Maintain two graphs:
A. Logical net graph: components/pins grouped by intended net.
B. Physical conductive graph: breadboard holes, internal strips, jumpers, component leads and terminal assignments.
Important nuance: A resistor/LED/transistor is NOT a zero-ohm jumper. When checking whether nets have shorted, do not union across functional component terminals. Union only ideal conductive links such as metal breadboard strips and jumper wires; map each device terminal into its appropriate physical conductive group. Device components connect nets through their electrical behavior, not by collapsing them into one net.

======================================================================
6. BREADBOARD MODEL AND COORDINATES
======================================================================
Represent each physical hole with a unique ID, e.g. A1-E63, F1-J63, and explicit rail IDs. On typical terminal strips, A3-E3 form one conductive group and F3-J3 another; the center trench separates them. A3 and A4 are not connected by default. Power rails may be split and MUST be explicitly modeled per verified board model.

Board model JSON should include:
- model_id and revision
- image/template geometry, physical pitch, rows and columns
- hole IDs and pixel/reference coordinates
- conductive_group_id for each hole
- center trench and keep-out regions
- rail segment definitions and polarity labels (labels are not electrical guarantees)
- supported component footprints and orientations

Start with one physically verified board. The name '830-point' alone does not guarantee identical rail continuity. Include a board topology test fixture.

Board API utilities:
get_hole(id), group_for_hole(id), are_directly_connected(a,b), holes_in_group(group), valid_footprint_placements(component), pixel_to_hole(image_point, calibration), hole_to_pixel(hole, calibration).

======================================================================
7. PLACEMENT AND WIRE ROUTING ALGORITHM
======================================================================
Goal: Map each logical pin to a physical hole and add jumpers so the resulting physical conductive groups correspond exactly to logical nets.

Hard constraints:
1. All selected holes exist on the chosen board model.
2. Pin footprints, spacing, polarity, and orientation are physically valid.
3. No incompatible leads occupy the same hole.
4. All pins assigned to a logical net become electrically connected by strips/jumpers.
5. Pins assigned to different logical nets are never inadvertently merged.
6. Rails, power terminals, and supply polarity are consistent.
7. Required clearance and supported wiring assumptions are satisfied.

Planner v1 (deterministic heuristic):
1. Parse and validate logical netlist.
2. Enumerate valid footprints for each component.
3. Place constrained components first (buttons spanning trench, polarity-sensitive devices, large footprints).
4. Place simpler two-pin components near related nets.
5. For each logical net, identify physical conductive groups containing its terminals.
6. Route jumpers between disconnected groups, selecting free holes and minimizing wire count/length; use Manhattan/Euclidean approximations initially.
7. Use union-find over ideal conductors to build actual conductive groups.
8. Verify every logical net is connected and distinct logical nets remain distinct.
9. Reject invalid layouts; backtrack or try alternative seeded placements.
10. Score valid layouts and produce numbered instructions.

Suggested objective after correctness:
J = alpha*(total_wire_length) + beta*(number_of_jumpers) + gamma*(wire_crossings) + delta*(assembly_difficulty).

Planner v2: OR-Tools CP-SAT for candidate footprint selection and occupancy, with a separate routing phase. Keep a deterministic baseline for comparison.

Required output for every plan:
- versioned circuit ID and board model ID
- component positions and pin->hole mappings
- jumper endpoints, optional color and length suggestion
- terminal-to-rail wiring assignments
- ordered assembly steps and rendered overlay coordinates
- validation report, warnings, and explicit unsupported cases

Never return an unvalidated layout as ready-to-build. If no valid layout is found, return a clear failure and reason.

9. API CONTRACT (FASTAPI)
======================================================================
GET    /api/v1/health                      -> status and version
POST   /api/v1/projects                    -> create project
GET    /api/v1/projects/{id}               -> retrieve owned project
POST   /api/v1/projects/{id}/circuits      -> validate/import versioned netlist
POST   /api/v1/circuits/validate           -> validate JSON without saving
GET    /api/v1/board-models                -> list supported board models
POST   /api/v1/plans                       -> generate validated assembly plan
GET    /api/v1/plans/{id}                  -> plan and validation report
GET    /api/v1/plans/{id}/steps            -> ordered wiring instructions
POST   /api/v1/images/uploads             -> signed upload URL
POST   /api/v1/verification-runs           -> submit plan ID + image ID
GET    /api/v1/verification-runs/{id}      -> observations and uncertainties
POST   /api/v1/assistant/messages         -> contextual explanations (optional)

Return Pydantic-validated JSON and consistent error responses. Use 422 for invalid schemas, 404 for missing/inaccessible resources, 409 for conflicting states. Require authentication/authorization on user resources once accounts are implemented. Use background jobs for slow image analysis rather than blocking requests.

Example plan request:
{"circuit_version_id":"<uuid>","board_model_id":"bb830-v1","preferences":{"minimize_wire_length":true,"minimize_crossings":true}}

Example verification output:
{"status":"needs_confirmation","electrically_verified":false,"observations":[{"type":"possible_missing_wire","expected_from":"A2","expected_to":"POWER_POS_1","confidence":0.73}]}

Do not mistake confidence scores for calibrated probabilities without calibration/testing. Never report visual inspection as electrical continuity proof.

11. COMPUTER VISION AND ML ROADMAP
======================================================================
Stage A — No trained model required:
- Require near-top-down images of a supported board.
- Use OpenCV to find board corners or printed fiducial markers.
- Estimate homography; map board holes to image pixels.
- Overlay the planned positions and allow manual calibration.

Stage B — Component detection:
- Fine-tune an existing YOLO-family detector for resistor, LED, button, wire endpoint, and board outline.
- Annotate bounding boxes plus pin/keypoint positions where feasible.
- Collect 300-500 initial labeled photos across different boards, lighting, camera angles, and circuits as a starting target, NOT an assurance of accuracy.
- Train/validation/test split 70/15/15 by physical circuit setup and capture session, avoiding near-duplicate leakage.

Stage C — Placement verification:
- Match detected components and endpoints to expected board coordinates.
- Produce classes: expected placement observed, possible wrong hole, possible missing component/wire, occluded/uncertain.
- Validate observations against the expected netlist and planned layout.
- Require user confirmation for low-confidence or occluded connections.
- Track false positives, false negatives, endpoint localization error, per-class precision/recall, and confidence calibration.

Stage D — Advanced (not initial MVP): multi-view reconstruction, wire segmentation, temporal video guidance, arbitrary physical-circuit-to-netlist reconstruction.

AI/LLM use:
- Explain each validated assembly step in natural language.
- Answer conceptual electronics questions.
- Describe uncertainty and ask clarifying questions.
- Never directly invent or overwrite pin/hole coordinates without calling the planner and validator.

12. TESTING AND RELEASE GATES
======================================================================
Unit tests:
- Breadboard A3-E3 connected; E3-F3 disconnected; A3-A4 disconnected.
- Split rails correctly modeled.
- Unknown holes rejected.
- Duplicate pin/net references rejected.
- Incorrect LED orientation rejected where orientation is specified.
- Functional device terminals are not incorrectly unioned as ideal conductors.
- Missing required jumper detected.
- Short between distinct logical nets detected.
- Generated plan always passes the validator or is rejected.

Property-based tests (recommended): randomly generate small supported circuits and check invariants, including no merged distinct nets, no overlapping occupied holes, and deterministic seeded results.

Integration tests:
- POST netlist -> validate -> plan -> retrieve steps.
- Frontend renders all returned coordinates on correct holes.
- Invalid netlist returns understandable errors.
- Project access is isolated by user.

Vision evaluation:
- Hold out entire circuit setups and photo sessions.
- Measure hole localization, detection mAP, endpoint accuracy, mismatch precision/recall, and rate of uncertain observations.

Release targets (aspirational, not achieved):
- 100% of released supported layouts pass deterministic connectivity validation.
- >=95% hole localization within one hole spacing in supported conditions.
- >=90% component detection mAP@50 on held-out supported images.
- >=30% assembly time reduction in controlled pilot, compared with ordinary instructions.

Competitive positioning: do not try to replace Fritzing, Tinkercad Circuits, or Wokwi. Start by complementing existing design/simulation workflows with physical build guidance and verification. Research competitors, API availability, licensing, and trademarks before launch.

16. ENGINEERING RISKS AND MITIGATIONS
======================================================================
Risk: hallucinated or invalid wiring instructions.
Mitigation: graph validator as hard release gate; AI assistant can only explain verified steps.

Risk: hidden/occluded wires cause false confidence.
Mitigation: ask for multiple views/manual confirmation; use 'uncertain' status.

Risk: breadboard rail geometry differs across manufacturers.
Mitigation: explicit verified board-model catalog and user board selection.

Risk: large scope stalls delivery.
Mitigation: one board, small circuit library, structured import, no live AR initially.

Risk: training data too small or unrealistic.
Mitigation: real held-out evaluation, active error collection, transparent metrics.

Risk: student willingness to pay is weak.
Mitigation: early behavioral validation and classroom pilots before expensive scaling.

Risk: privacy and security of uploaded images.
Mitigation: signed uploads, access controls, deletion, limited retention, opt-in training consent.
